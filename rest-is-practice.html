Rest is part of the practice.

Rest is not what happens when the meaningful work is finished. It is part of what makes meaningful work possible.

Yoga Chapters · November 2026

We often treat rest as a reward: something earned after enough work, movement, service, or achievement. Under that rule, rest is always postponed because there is always something else that could be done.

Yoga offers another view. Effort and ease are not opponents. They are partners. A sustainable practice learns when to engage and when to release.

Rest does not interrupt your progress. It gives your progress somewhere to settle.

Listen before the body has to shout

The need for rest rarely arrives as a dramatic message. It first appears as small signals: irritability, scattered attention, heavy movement, or the feeling that every ordinary task requires negotiation.

Listening early is not weakness. It is skill. The better we notice these signals, the less often exhaustion has to make the decision for us.

A five-minute resting practice

  1. Lie down or sit with your back supported.
  2. Place one hand on the chest and one on the abdomen.
  3. Let the eyes close or soften.
  4. Feel five breaths without changing them.
  5. Ask: “What would support me next?” and listen before answering.

Let rest be complete

Rest becomes less restorative when it is filled with apology, scrolling, or planning the next task. Even a short pause can be complete when you allow it to be the only thing happening.

The practice is not to become endlessly calm. It is to build an honest relationship with your energy. Some days ask for movement. Some days ask for stillness. Wisdom is learning the difference.
